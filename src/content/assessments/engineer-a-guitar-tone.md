This is the course's integrating assessment: everything from string to
listener, brought together and pointed at a single design goal.

## The task

Starting from a target tonal description — either one given to you, or one
you state clearly yourself, for example:

> Design a saturated lead tone with strong sustain, clear note definition,
> controlled low frequencies, and sufficient midrange presence.

design a complete signal chain and justify it. Your design should cover, as
relevant to your target:

- source and string assumptions
- pickup
- guitar controls
- filtering
- gain
- clipping
- modulation
- delay
- reverb
- amplifier
- speaker and cabinet

## What counts as a sufficient justification

An insufficient justification names a preference without a mechanism:

> "I selected this because it sounds better."

A sufficient justification connects a specific decision to a specific
consequence, using the course's causal-chain reasoning:

> "The midrange emphasis is applied before the clipping stage so that these
> frequencies drive the nonlinear stage more strongly and therefore influence
> both the original spectrum and the newly generated harmonics."

Every major decision in your design should read like the second example, not
the first.

## Skills assessed

Integration; system design; technical justification; understanding of
interactions between stages; recognition of trade-offs.
