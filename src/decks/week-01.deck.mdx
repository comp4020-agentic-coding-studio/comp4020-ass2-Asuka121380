# What do we mean when we say two guitars have different "tone"?

Not "warm" or "bright". What actually changed, physically?

---

## Three properties, one word

"Tone" bundles together three separate things.

**Pitch** — fundamental frequency.
**Loudness** — amplitude.
**Timbre** — everything else.

---

## Timbre is a composite

No single number captures it. Timbre comes from:

- harmonic balance
- attack and decay
- modulation over time
- nonlinear distortion
- resonances in the instrument and chain

---

## Two views of the same signal

```text
Time domain   → waveform  → amplitude vs. time
Frequency domain → spectrum → energy vs. frequency
```

Neither is more "real". This course moves between both, constantly.

---

## The guitar signal chain

```text
String → Pickup → Electronics → Pedals → Amplifier → Speaker → Room → Listener
```

Every stage can reshape the signal. Tone belongs to the whole chain, not
any one link.

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## Hear it: same pitch, three timbres

Sine, square, and sawtooth — all at 220 Hz.

Same fundamental. Same pitch. Completely different sound.

---

## Why they differ

A square and a sawtooth carry harmonics a sine doesn't.

Same fundamental frequency, different harmonic balance — that difference
*is* the timbre you hear.

---

## Interactive example

Open the Week 1 lab bench on this week's page.

Switch between sine, square, and sawtooth at the same pitch. Watch the

---

## Main conclusions

- Pitch, loudness, and timbre are separate, measurable properties.
- Waveform and spectrum are two readings of one signal, not two signals.
- Tone is a property of the whole signal chain, not any single component.
