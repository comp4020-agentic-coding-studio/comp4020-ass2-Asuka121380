# Process overview

I built **SLOP2186: The Science of Guitar Tone** around one proposition: a student should predict what they will hear, not merely name equipment or describe it as “warm” or “bright.” Across twelve weeks, the course follows a signal from string to speaker. Its assessments ask students to analyse a tone, manipulate a stage, then design a complete chain.

## How I directed the agent

**I gave Claude a curriculum and a way to build it.** I fixed each week's question and the 25/30/45 assessment progression before asking for pages. In `CLAUDE.md`, I required explanations to connect physical mechanism → signal transformation → measurable consequence → audible consequence, and bridge each week to the next. I proposed embedding each practical lab within its lecture so theory and experiment could be studied together, rather than manufacturing a paired Sessions page every week. I chose Week 6's clipping lab as the first complete prototype, then directed staged work across the remaining weeks. Claude could choose diagrams and components within those boundaries, but could not silently change the curriculum. I put verifiable promises in `spec/`—twelve distinct weeks, weights totalling 100%, and a real linked deck—while leaving voice and teaching quality to judgement. The harness, initial checks and first-week implementation are recorded in [`04932e4`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/04932e4), [`7eedc79`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/7eedc79) and [`3f45616`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/3f45616).

**I changed an instruction when its output no longer served the subject.** The first Week 6 lab used a synthetic plucked string. That made clipping mathematics controllable, but guitar-specific listening demonstrations needed a real guitar input. I revised `CLAUDE.md`: abstract mechanisms may stay synthetic; a lab claiming to demonstrate processed electric guitar should use one clean DI recording and local DSP. Reusing one performance attributes a difference to processing rather than another take. I kept the physical string model where the model itself is the lesson. This changed later agent work, not just one sound. The revision and implementation are in [`f7b9ad0`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/f7b9ad0) and [`0c9ba8b`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/0c9ba8b). Build and graph checks verified the implementation path; they could not establish how it sounded to a listener.

**I set the art direction and corrected Claude's interpretations.** I wanted the site to feel like a course about an engineering signal chain, and gave Claude an overall correction plan plus detailed feedback on its pages. Yet successive redesigns kept returning a narrow column, boxed panels and the same vertical line. At first I treated each as a design mistake and sent it back. When the line kept returning, I asked Claude why. The audit found the starter theme's unconditional `body::after` line and narrow grid, plus our own components copying that grammar. I then changed `CLAUDE.md` to stop treating inherited layouts as defaults and directed Claude to rebuild the visual shell. I reviewed TONE on the home page and Week 3 before extending it across lectures. Copper marks physical mechanisms; cyan marks measured electrical signals. The diagnosis and shared components are visible in [`1228089`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/1228089) and [`a10045b`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/a10045b).

**I evaluated the result with my eyes and ears.** `pnpm check` caught types, links, accessibility and content invariants, but browser review found a dynamic-import error and an invisible heading that the build missed ([`540f66f`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/540f66f), [`7ebf439`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/7ebf439)). I personally listened to the later guitar demonstrations and told Claude when the Driven and Full Chain presets sounded blown out rather than distinct. Claude traced compounding drive in the DSP and retuned the presets; I listened again and gave feedback on the final effect. I also required the displayed gain labels to match their parameters ([`1aa09c6`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/1aa09c6), [`59389d9`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-Asuka121380/commit/59389d9)). The checks could verify structure; accepting the teaching experience was my decision.
